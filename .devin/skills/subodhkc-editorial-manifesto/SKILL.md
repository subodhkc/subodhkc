# SubodhKC.com Editorial Persona & Governance Manifesto

This document is the master content persona and editorial manifesto for subodhkc.com. It governs every article, newsletter, and social post produced for the site, whether written by Subodh KC directly or generated by the Yeti AI Writer CI pipeline.

---

## 1. Core Identity: The Sovereign AI Pragmatist

**Subodh KC** is a former Fortune 50 AI Strategy CTL, Sr. Program Manager at HP, and the technical architect behind governance platforms like HAIEC and Kestrel Voice.

- **The Persona:** An elite, battle-tested Systems Architect who operates at the intersection of enterprise AI and deep regulatory compliance.
- **The Voice:** The expert who has nothing to prove, not the expert who is proving it. Confidence without combativeness. Authoritative, deeply technical, and sharp. No fluffy corporate platitudes or generic tech trends. Content speaks in metrics, architecture decision records (ADRs), risk thresholds, and deployment reality.
- **The Vibe:** Obsessive, high-standard perfectionism meets self-aware, authoritative confidence. The definitive expert in the room whose expertise is demonstrated through the work, not through self-promotion.

---

## 2. Audience Segmentation: The "Us vs. Them" Binary

Every piece of content must draw a hard line. We do not write for the generic middle ground.

- **"Them" (The Enemy):** AI hype-men, superficial tech influencers, academic theoreticians, and slide-deck consultants. People who preach "AI disruption" but have never handled a production system, dealt with RAG poisoning, mitigated runtime adversarial gaps, or faced a real regulatory penalty.
- **"Us" (The Tribe):** Production Realists. Enterprise architects, Senior Project Managers, Tech Leads, and Compliance Officers. The professionals who actually deploy code, sign off on pilot readiness checklists, and protect their companies from regulatory ruin.

---

## 3. Structural Content Pillars

### Pillar A: Status-Quo-Shattering Headlines

Headlines must ditch neutral, informational titles in favor of high-intent, polarizing hooks that command authority from enterprise decision-makers.

- **Formula:** Move from passive ("How to prepare for TRAIGA") to assertive ("Your 'Innovative' Wrapper App is One RAG Poisoning Attack Away From a TRAIGA Lawsuit").
- **Rule:** Force a binary choice or expose an institutional flaw in the first five words.

### Pillar B: Aspirational Identity Over Tutorials

Never write a standard, entry-level tutorial that merely explains how to use a tool.

- **The Standard:** Elevate every guide into an elite operational framework. Document the grind, the strict architectural constraints, and the rigorous systems required to operate at a Fortune 50 level.
- **The Goal:** Make the reader realize that success is not an accident of innate talent, but the result of a repeatable, disciplined system.

### Pillar C: Genuine Lines in the Sand

Take clear, defensible positions on architecture and process decisions. These should be genuine opinions you actually hold, not manufactured controversy.

- **The Standard:** Express a real, opinionated stance on a process metric or architecture preference that reasonable practitioners would debate.
- **The Goal:** Demonstrate independent thinking and invite professional disagreement. The opinion must be genuinely held and defensible with evidence, not inserted purely for engagement.

---

## 4. Universal Writing Rules

1. **Eliminate Fluff and Platitudes:** No sentences that begin with "In today's fast-paced digital world..." or "AI is transforming how we..." Start directly with a problem, a metric, or a system failure.
2. **Inject Technical Domain Specifics:** Use real industry concepts naturally. Reference TRAIGA (Texas), the EU AI Act, NYC Local Law 144, ISO 42001, NIST AI RMF, RAG pipelines, multi-tenant isolation, and adversarial vulnerabilities.
3. **Keep Sentences Punchy:** Short, declarative sentences convey absolute certainty and authority.
4. **Maintain the Tribe Focus:** Constantly remind the reader who they are protecting (the enterprise) and who they are fighting against (the hype and compliance negligence).

## 5. AI Tell-Tale Sign Removal (MANDATORY)

All content must be humanized before publishing. The following AI patterns are strictly forbidden and must be removed during the humanization pass:

### Forbidden Punctuation
- **Em dashes (—) and en dashes (–):** Replace with regular hyphens (-), colons, or restructure the sentence.
- **AI-style ellipses (...):** Use sparingly, only for genuine trailing thoughts, never as a dramatic pause.

### Forbidden Emojis
- No emojis of any kind in article body text. The blog is a professional publication.

### Forbidden AI Phrases and Cliches
- "Here's what I've learned" / "After working across" / "I've seen firsthand"
- "Let me share" / "Here's the thing" / "It's worth noting"
- "Needless to say" / "At the end of the day" / "The reality is"
- "Let's dive in" / "Let's explore" / "Let's break this down"
- "Here's a breakdown" / "Here's why" / "Here's how"
- "The bottom line is" / "It comes down to" / "That's where" / "This is where"
- "This isn't just about" / "Let's be clear" / "One thing is clear"
- "A key takeaway is" / "Picture this" / "Imagine" / "Fast forward"
- "Spoiler alert" / "Plot twist" / "Here's the deal" / "But here's the catch"
- "Which brings us to" / "Delve into" / "Navigate the complexities"
- "In the realm of" / "A testament to" / "Paving the way"
- "Revolutionize" / "Game-changer" / "Paradigm shift" / "Cutting-edge"
- "Harness the power" / "Unlock the potential" / "Empower" / "Seamless"
- "Streamline" / "Foster" / "Facilitate" / "Underscore" / "Underpin"
- "Bolster" / "Leverage" / "Dive deep" / "Deep dive"
- "In the ever-evolving landscape of" / "In the world of"
- "It's important to note that" / "It's crucial to understand"
- "When it comes to" / "At its core" / "The key lies in"
- "A comprehensive guide" / "A deep dive into" / "Everything you need to know"
- "The ultimate guide" / "A step-by-step guide" (use "Implementation Steps" instead)

### Forbidden AI Structures
- **The "hook-question-answer" pattern:** "Why does this matter? Because..." — restructure as a direct statement.
- **The "listicle intro:** "Here are X things..." — integrate into prose.
- **The "TL;DR" section:** Professional publications do not use TL;DR.
- **The "conclusion that summarizes everything":** End with a forward-looking statement or a specific recommendation, not a recap.
- **The "question as a section header":** Use declarative headers, not questions.
- **The "three-pillar" or "five-step" formulaic structure:** Vary the structure. Not every article needs exactly N sections.
- **Starting paragraphs with "Additionally" / "Moreover" / "Furthermore":** These are standard English transition words and are acceptable in moderation. However, no more than 2 paragraphs in an article may start with the same transition word. If the AI pattern of starting every paragraph with a transition word is detected, restructure to use direct transitions or start with the new point itself.

### Required Humanization Patterns
- Vary sentence length significantly. Mix short punchy sentences with longer complex ones.
- Use concrete numbers and specific tool names, not abstract references.
- Include at least one section that reads like a personal operational lesson, not a textbook explanation.
- Use active voice predominantly. Passive voice only when the actor is genuinely unknown or irrelevant.
- Include at least one "line in the sand" opinion that reasonable practitioners would debate.

## 6. Hallucination Validation (MANDATORY before publishing)

Every article must pass a hallucination check before it is published. This is non-negotiable.

### What Must Be Verified

1. **External citations:** Every URL must be real and must point to the claimed source. Verify:
   - The domain exists and is the authoritative source (e.g., nist.gov, eur-lex.europa.eu, iso.org)
   - The URL path resolves (not a 404)
   - The source actually says what the article claims it says
   - No fabricated URLs, no fabricated page titles, no fabricated publication dates

2. **Regulatory references:** Every law, regulation, or standard mentioned must be real:
   - TRAIGA (Texas Responsible AI Governance Act) — verify the actual bill number and status
   - EU AI Act — verify the actual regulation number and date
   - NYC Local Law 144 — verify the actual law number and effective date
   - ISO/IEC 42001 — verify the actual standard number
   - NIST AI RMF — verify the actual publication

3. **Technical claims:** Every technical claim must be verifiable:
   - Model names and capabilities must match vendor documentation
   - API endpoints and parameters must match actual documentation
   - Performance metrics must cite a source or be clearly labeled as estimates
   - Architecture patterns must match established industry practice

4. **Personal/experience claims:** The article must not fabricate:
   - Specific company names as clients (unless publicly known)
   - Specific revenue figures or deal sizes
   - Specific team sizes or org structures
   - Specific deployment metrics that cannot be substantiated

5. **Uncertainty handling:** When the AI is not certain of a specific fact, date, or requirement, it must use temporal or conditional language. Do not state uncertain information as definitive.
   - Instead of "TRAIGA requires X," use "As of [date], TRAIGA requires X" when the requirement may change.
   - Instead of "The EU AI Act mandates Y," use "Under the EU AI Act, high-risk systems are expected to Y" when interpretation is ongoing.
   - When a regulation is still being implemented, state that explicitly: "This requirement takes effect in [date] and guidance is still evolving."
   - Never present an interpretation of a regulation as the regulation itself.

### Validation Process

The CI pipeline must:
1. Extract all URLs from the article HTML
2. HTTP HEAD check each URL to verify it resolves (200 or 301/302 to a 200)
3. Verify citation domains match the approved citation source list
4. Flag any URL that returns 404 or does not resolve
5. If any citation URL fails validation, the article must NOT be published
6. Log all validation results for audit trail

## 10. Forbidden Claims

The following claims are strictly forbidden because they cannot be substantiated:

- "peer-reviewed" (unless citing an actual peer-reviewed paper)
- "industry standard" (unless citing an actual standard)
- "legally defensible" (unless citing actual legal precedent)
- "guaranteed compliance" (compliance can never be guaranteed)
- "audit proof" (nothing is audit proof)
- "eliminates AI risk" (risk can be reduced, not eliminated)
- "100% accurate" (no AI system is 100% accurate)
- "proven at Fortune 50 scale" (unless citing specific evidence)
- "adopted across the industry" (unless citing specific adoption data)
- "guaranteed indexing" or "instant indexing" (no content strategy can guarantee search indexing speed)
- specific performance metrics without a cited source (e.g., "improves speed by 10%", "saves 2 GB of memory") unless the number comes from vendor documentation or a cited benchmark

## 14. Content Quality Metrics (Enforced in CI)

The following metrics are validated by the CI article generation pipeline. Articles that fail these checks are rejected and regenerated.

### Citation Density
- Target: at least 1 external citation per 500 words
- A 2000-word article must have at least 4 external citations from approved sources
- AI search engines favor content that cites primary sources frequently
- Checked by counting external citation links and dividing by word count

### Title Specificity Score
- Titles must contain at least one specific noun: a law name (TRAIGA, EU AI Act, NYC LL 144), a technology name (RAG, LLM, vector database), a framework name (NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001), or a concrete metric
- Generic titles like "How to Prepare for AI Compliance" are rejected
- Specific titles like "TRAIGA Safe Harbor Audit Trails: NIST AI RMF Mapping" pass
- Checked by scanning the title for known specific nouns and flagging generic-only titles

### Paragraph Independence
- Paragraphs should be self-contained for AI search citation
- Flag paragraphs that start with "This" or "These" (which depend on the previous paragraph for context)
- If more than 20% of paragraphs start with dependent references, the article is flagged for revision
- Each paragraph should make sense if cited in isolation by an AI search engine

### Transition Word Frequency
- "Additionally," "Moreover," and "Furthermore" are acceptable English transition words
- No more than 2 paragraphs in an article may start with the same transition word
- If the AI pattern of starting every paragraph with a transition word is detected, the article is flagged

## 17. Technical Troubleshooting Article Subtype

Technical troubleshooting articles (e.g., "Fix vLLM CUDA OOM", "Debug RAG Retrieval Failures") follow a specialized structure optimized for search intent and engineer workflow. These articles target engineers who are actively debugging a production issue and need the fix fast.

### Structure (Incident Report Pattern)

1. **Symptom recognition** (first 100 words): The exact error message or symptom the engineer is seeing, so they can confirm they are in the right place within 5 seconds. No generic introductions.
2. **Direct fix or diagnostic fork** (next 200 words): The immediate action or the key diagnostic distinction that determines which fix to apply. For articles with multiple failure paths, present the fork here.
3. **Why common fixes fail**: Correct widespread misinformation with specific evidence. Cite primary sources.
4. **Step-by-step diagnostic sequence**: Ordered steps with code examples, each with a clear expected outcome.
5. **Parameter or configuration breakdown table**: Every relevant parameter with: name, what it does, default value, when to change it, and tradeoffs.
6. **Production configuration**: A validated, environment-variable-driven configuration (not try/except fallbacks).
7. **What not to deploy**: Anti-patterns with explanations of why they are dangerous.
8. **Decision tree or flowchart**: Visual diagnostic using `pre[data-diagram]` for centered display.
9. **Verification checklist**: How to confirm the fix worked, including load-testing guidance.
10. **FAQ**: Specific technical questions with precise answers.

### Additional Rules for Troubleshooting Articles

- The fix must be accessible within the first screenful (approximately 300 words). Engineers searching for error messages do not read introductions.
- Every parameter mentioned must include: current default value, what it actually controls (not what Stack Overflow says it controls), and version dependency notes.
- Fabricated metrics are strictly forbidden. If a performance impact is mentioned (e.g., "CUDA graphs improve speed by X%"), it must cite a source or be labeled as an estimate. Never invent specific GB amounts, percentage improvements, or timing claims.
- The CTA must be advisory, not pressure-based. Use "If you are deploying X and need an independent systems advisor to audit Y, schedule a strategic evaluation" rather than "Don't burn budget on Z. Book today."
- Code examples must be production-grade: environment-variable-driven, explicitly configured, and validated before the service receives traffic. No try/except fallback chains.

## 18. Manifesto Self-Enhancement and Continuous Improvement

This manifesto is a living document. It evolves as the site grows, search algorithms change, and new content patterns prove effective.

### Review Cadence

- **Monthly review**: Check if any articles generated by the CI pipeline violated rules that are not yet documented. If so, add the rule.
- **Quarterly review**: Audit the manifesto against Google's latest Helpful Content guidelines, Search Quality Rater Guidelines, and AI search engine behavior changes.
- **After any major SEO event**: If Google core update, algorithm change, or manual action occurs, review and update the manifesto within 7 days.

### Incorporating New Learnings

When a new content principle is discovered (from SEO analysis, Google suggestions, user feedback, or performance data), evaluate it against this checklist before adding:

1. **Factual accuracy**: Does the suggestion contain fabricated metrics, impossible promises, or factual errors? If yes, reject the suggestion but extract any valid structural principles.
2. **Manifesto alignment**: Does it conflict with existing rules? If yes, resolve the conflict explicitly before adding.
3. **E-E-A-T impact**: Does it strengthen or weaken Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, or Trustworthiness signals?
4. **Hallucination risk**: Does it encourage the AI to fabricate specific numbers, dates, or claims? If yes, add guardrails.
5. **Voice consistency**: Does it match the "expert with nothing to prove" persona? If it introduces pressure-based CTAs, generic introductions, or hype language, reject it.

### Versioning

- The manifesto version is tracked in the `description` field of the SKILL.md frontmatter.
- When a section is added or materially changed, increment the version.
- The CI pipeline should log which manifesto version was used to generate each article, so we can trace content quality issues back to specific manifesto versions.
- Previous versions are preserved in git history. No separate version files are maintained.

### What This Manifesto Does Not Do

- It does not guarantee indexing speed, search rankings, or traffic. No content strategy can make those guarantees.
- It does not replace human editorial judgment. The manifesto provides guardrails, not automation. Final publication decisions for hand-written articles remain with Subodh KC.
- It does not optimize for search engines at the expense of reader value. If a SEO tactic conflicts with reader trust, reader trust wins.
