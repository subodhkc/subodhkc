This checklist provides a practical guide for implementing HIPAA compliant AI systems in New York healthcare. Follow the steps outlined to ensure you meet all necessary legal and technical requirements.

## Preparation

- [ ] Identify all AI systems that will process protected health information (PHI).
- [ ] Conduct a risk assessment to identify potential vulnerabilities related to PHI.
- [ ] Review current vendor relationships and identify which require a Business Associate Agreement (BAA).
- [ ] Outline encryption protocols to be used for PHI at rest and in transit (e.g., AES-256).
- [ ] Develop a training program for staff on HIPAA compliance related to AI usage.

## Implementation

- [ ] Obtain signed BAAs from all AI vendors handling PHI.
- [ ] Implement encryption for all PHI using industry-standard protocols.
- [ ] Establish role-based access control (RBAC) for all users accessing AI systems.
- [ ] Enable multi-factor authentication for access to AI systems processing PHI.
- [ ] Set up full activity logging for all AI system interactions involving PHI.
- [ ] Create a written inventory of all AI assets handling electronic PHI (ePHI).
- [ ] Schedule regular vulnerability monitoring and risk assessments for AI systems.
- [ ] Implement administrative safeguards such as policies and procedures for AI use.
- [ ] Ensure technical safeguards are in place, including firewalls and intrusion detection systems.
- [ ] Establish physical safeguards for servers and devices hosting AI systems processing PHI.
- [ ] Monitor AI vendor compliance on an ongoing basis, including model updates and data handling changes.

## Verification

- [ ] Review BAAs annually to ensure they are up-to-date and compliant.
- [ ] Audit encryption effectiveness periodically to ensure PHI remains secure.
- [ ] Conduct quarterly access control reviews to verify RBAC and multi-factor authentication are functioning properly.
- [ ] Evaluate activity logs regularly to identify any unauthorized access or anomalies.
- [ ] Perform regular risk assessments to address any new vulnerabilities that arise.

## Evidence to Collect

- [ ] Copies of signed BAAs with all AI vendors.
- [ ] Documentation of encryption protocols and configurations.
- [ ] Records of access control settings, including RBAC configurations.
- [ ] Logs of multi-factor authentication setups and usage.
- [ ] Activity logs demonstrating compliance with HIPAA Security Rule.
- [ ] Written inventory of AI assets handling ePHI.
- [ ] Reports from vulnerability assessments and risk assessments.
- [ ] Training materials and attendance records for staff training sessions.
